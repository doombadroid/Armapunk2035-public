No goggles, no radial. The app reads whatever your crosshair is on as the locked target (shown in the scope). A tile greys out with a reason (OUT OF RANGE, NO LOS, WRONG TARGET) so you never fire blind. Goggles, when worn, still add the fast SHIFT+Y ring on top; the app is the fallback that always works.

Two things to react to: the loadout-in-app model (do you want a fixed 6 like the ring, or a bigger scrollable set since the app has room?), and the target scope (cursor-lock readout vs. a tap-to-pick list of nearby targets). Everything below is one system, recolored per side.

The pattern, both sides

Cursor is the target

The scope mirrors cursorTarget live. Aim at a cruiser, a suspect, an ATM, a door. Area hacks (Jam, Crybaby) fire where you look and don't need a lock.

Tile carries its own rules

Each tile shows upload, cooldown and range, and self-disables with the reason. Uploading shows the bar; cooling shows the timer. What's castable reads at a glance.

Loadout is yours

The library holds everything you own. Load the six you want; they persist. TAL OS gates its rack by rank, so a Cadet sees fewer live tiles than a Captain.

One chassis, two identities

Same layout, same status strip, same interactions. ICEBREAKER is illegal-acid; TAL OS is issued-amber. You always know which side you're holding.
